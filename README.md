# Raphaela: a blog about life, balance, psychology, astrology, the stars and faith

React (Vite) + Fastify + MySQL, all TypeScript. Romanian by default, English available.

- `client/`: React 19 + React Router (data router) + TipTap editor, on http://localhost:5173 (proxies `/api` and `/uploads`)
- `server/`: Fastify 5 (run with `tsx`) on http://localhost:3000
- MySQL 8.4 in Docker (`docker-compose.yml`)

Both packages import with the `@/*` alias for `src/*` (see each `tsconfig.json`).

## Features

- **Sign-in:** "Continue with Google" is the highlighted option on `/login` and `/register`; email + password is the secondary option. After the first Google sign-in, a modal asks whether to keep the Google name or pick another display name. On phones (640px and narrower) both pages are a full screen of their own, like an app's sign-in: no back link, settings or account buttons, no card, the fields named by their placeholders (the labels stay for screen readers), and nothing to scroll down to a 375 × 553 screen.
- **Terms and Conditions:** creating an account needs agreeing to them. On `/register` it's a checkbox that both "Continue with Google" and the email form wait for (the server also refuses an email sign-up without it). A Google account created anywhere else (the login page, the discussion's sign-in card, the settings menu) agrees in that first sign-in modal; the other choice there is signing out. When someone agreed is stored (`users.terms_accepted_at`). Accounts that existed before aren't asked. `/login` says that continuing means agreeing.
- **Access:**
  - **Guests** read the home page, posts, the terms page and their saved posts (`/articole-salvate`), and can like and save posts (kept in `localStorage`).
  - **Members** also comment, reply and react to comments. Their likes and saved posts are stored in the account, and whatever they saved as a guest moves in when they sign in.
  - **Authors** also write posts, and edit or delete their own (`/{username}/articole` lists them). They can preview their own drafts and scheduled posts.
  - **Owners** edit and delete any post (their own `/{username}/articole` lists every post), write only in an author's name (see below), manage categories, give or take the author role (`/admin/users`), remove anyone's comment, and still see deleted posts and comments, marked "Șters".
  - Accounts whose email is in `OWNER_EMAILS` become owners when they sign in; that role isn't changed from the Users page. The server enforces all of this; the UI only hides what you can't do.
- **Profile** (`/profil`, from the account menu): display name, username and profile picture. Authors and owners also set an occupation (shown instead of "Autor" on their posts) and a personal quote (shown under their name at the end of their posts), with a live preview beside the form; members' page is one column in the middle, under a centred title. On phones, Save spans the form.
  - **Username:** in the addresses of their pages; made from the name at sign-up ("Victor Solaris" → `victor.solaris`). Lowercase letters and digits, words joined by single dots (not at the start or the end), 3–30, not one of the site's own words (`server/src/lib/usernames.ts`). As it's typed it becomes lowercase, loses diacritics, and spaces, hyphens and underscores turn into dots; 2 seconds after the last key the page asks whether it's free (`GET /api/me/username-check`) and says so under the field ("Liber", taken, or what's wrong with it); Save waits while it's taken or invalid.
  - **Picture:** JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF of at least 256 × 256 px (smaller ones are explained in a dialog, and refused by the server too), framed in a crop dialog with the circle that's kept and previews at the sizes the site shows (profile, comments, posts); saved as a 512 × 512 square at most (WebP on the server). "Remove" goes back to the Google picture or initials.
  - **Account and sign-in** (a card under the profile form): the email, Google and the password, each with its dialog. A new email asks for the current password (or, for an account made with Google, a new one, since it can't sign in with Google after) and disconnects Google, whose address is always the account's; the dialog says so. "Conectează Google" goes to Google's account chooser (`/api/auth/google?link=1`): an account with the same address is connected at once, one with another address comes back to a dialog showing the change ("from … to …") and is only connected, with the email changed, once confirmed (it waits with the session for 15 minutes, `sessions.google_link`). "Alt cont" replaces the connected Google account the same way, after a warning. "Deconectează" asks first and, without a password, has one chosen in the dialog. A new password asks for the current one and signs out the account's other browsers. A Google account already connected to someone else, or whose address another account has, is refused with the reason. Emails aren't checked by sending a message (there's no email service yet), so an administrator address (`OWNER_EMAILS`) can't be typed in, at sign-up or as a new email: only Google, which proves it.
- **Top bar:** no bar, just a settings cog and the account menu in the top-right corner. The cog popover has Write, Posts and Stats (authors: their own pages), theme, language, and sign-in for guests. The account menu has Profile, Settings, Saved, Support and Sign out; an administrator's has Profile, Settings, Administrare and Sign out (their cog: theme and language).
- **Top bar on narrow screens** (1000px and narrower, phones and tablets, where the post page has its bottom bar): the cog and the account menu are one button, the profile picture (the cog for guests), which drops a sheet from the top, swiped up to close like the share sheet is swiped down: who's signed in (or the way to sign in), Profile, Settings and Saved, the writing pages for authors (an administrator's sheet: Profile, Settings, Administrare and Sign out), theme, language, notifications (new posts on this device, or why this device can't get them), Sign out, and the footer's links.
- **Footer:** the site's social links and contact address, as icons (when set in the admin panel → Setări → Identitate), then © the company and the current year · Termeni și Condiții (`/termeni-si-conditii`) · Cookie-uri (opens the cookie banner again). Phones (640px and narrower) have no footer: the same line is at the bottom of the account sheet instead, centred.
- **Home:** a search bar (ignores case and diacritics) and a "Saved" pill above the latest posts. Posts an administrator pinned come first (marked "Fixat"), then the newest; the first one is the big card. The category tree and the newsletter box are in a right-hand sidebar, and filtering a category includes its sub-categories. A tag's page (`/eticheta/{slug}`) is the same list for that tag.
- **Summaries** (the home page, search results, link previews, the RSS feed, new-post notifications): what the author wrote in "Rezumat", or, when they left it empty, the start of the text, cut at a word under 160 characters. That one is made when shown (only what the author wrote is stored), so it follows the text as it's edited, and the editor's field stays empty.
- **Home on narrow screens** (1000px and narrower, like the top bar): a feed. The search moves into the top bar, beside its buttons; there are no categories and no Saved pill (Saved is in the account sheet). Posts follow one another, from edge to edge on phones as in social apps, as cards in a centred column on tablets: the author, how long ago ("3 h", then the date), the reading time and the category, then the title and excerpt, the banner across the screen, and Like, comments (to the discussion), Share, views and Save. More posts load on the way down. Authors and owners also get the post page's bottom bar with their tools: a round + (new post) in the middle, their posts on the left, statistics on the right (the dashboard for owners, `/statistici` for authors); members and guests don't.
- **Logo and splash:** the logo is `server/assets/logo.png` (a phoenix, transparent background), unless an administrator uploaded another one (Setări → Identitate; kept as `uploads/brand-logo.png`). The server makes everything else from it: the favicon, the home-screen icons (`/icons/*`, on the night sky where the launcher needs a background), the logo search engines get, and the share images' corner; their addresses carry the logo's version, so replacing the file updates them all (restart the dev servers after replacing it). Opening the site at its home page shows a splash for at least 3 seconds: the logo, the name and a filling line on the midnight sky (stars and a shooting star), then it fades once the posts are there. It's in `client/index.html` (so it shows at the first paint, before the app loads); `client/src/lib/splash.ts` takes it away. Other pages, and coming back to home inside the app, don't show it.
- **Midnight theme** (the default; a choice made in the settings is kept): a starry background with falling stars and glowing constellations (all 12 zodiac signs, Orion, Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, Cygnus) that draw in, hold and change every few seconds. It's disabled when the OS asks for reduced motion. The data is in `client/src/lib/constellations.ts`.
- **Likes** are public: anyone can like a post, no account needed. A guest's like belongs to their browser (a visitor cookie; a browser that lost its cookie is recognised by its fingerprint, see Statistics), and moves into their account when they sign in. One like per person and post; taking it back keeps the row, marked removed, so the history stays. Likes kept in a browser's `localStorage` before this are sent to the server once.
- **Saved posts** need an account to be stored on the server; a guest's stay in `localStorage` and move into the account on sign-in. A guest's first save shows a one-time toast suggesting an account. Saved posts are listed at `/{username}/articole-salvate` (a guest's at `/articole-salvate`; `/saved` leads there).
- **A person's pages:** `/{username}/articole` (their posts) and `/{username}/articole-salvate` (what they saved). Both start with who they are (picture, name, occupation or role, quote), their lists as tabs, and "Distribuie" while the list is public: the share sheet with the list's own share image (their picture, name and occupation; `/api/og/u/{username}/{list}.jpg`). Both lists are private until they're made public in Settings ("Confidențialitate": the posts for authors, the saved ones for everyone); private, others get "Lista aceasta e privată", and the owner a note with the way to Settings. Public article lists are indexed by search engines; saved lists aren't. There's no separate "public profile" switch: the name, picture, occupation and quote are already public on every post and comment. The person and administrators manage the articles on the same page (by status: all, published, scheduled, drafts, deleted for administrators), with "Articol nou". The API mirrors the addresses: `GET/POST /api/u/{username}/articole`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /api/u/{username}/articole/{id}`, `GET /api/u/{username}/articole-salvate`. The old `/admin/posts` and `/editor/…` addresses lead to the new ones.
- **Writing:** the editor is at `/{username}/articole/nou` and `/{username}/articole/{id}` (the address follows the post's author). Administrators never post as themselves: the editor's first setting is the author, required (a new post at an author's address starts with them, the administrator's own starts empty), and a post an administrator wrote before must get an author when it's next saved.
- **Posts:** rich editor with headings, text styles, colours and highlights, alignment, lists and checklists, quotes, code, links, images (button, paste or drag and drop), YouTube embeds and tables. Also a banner image, a category, an excerpt and a slug. The banner must be at least 1280 × 720 px (smaller ones get an explanation in a dialog); it's then framed to 16:9 in a crop dialog (drag to position; zoom with the wheel, the slider or +/−, never past the 1280 px minimum), scaled down to at most 1920 × 1080 (the largest the site shows it) if it's bigger, never up. Removing it asks first. Every uploaded image (banner, in-post, profile picture) must really be an image whatever the browser calls it (a GIF too), and is stored as WebP by the server (`sharp`, quality 90; WebP files that already fit are kept byte for byte), capped at 1920 px; uploads can be up to 25 MB, and bigger files are scaled down in the browser first. Opened with "Edit" from a post, the editor's back link returns to that post. A post can be **draft**, **published now** or **scheduled** (a future publish date; it appears automatically once that time passes). Comments can be turned off per post; if it already has comments, the editor warns that they'll be hidden (they come back if comments are turned on again). Deleting a post is a soft delete: it disappears from the site, and owners still see it on a "Șterse" tab. The HTML is sanitized on the server.
- **Autosave:** a draft saves itself about 4 seconds after the last change ("Salvat automat" in the top bar), so a new post becomes a draft without a click. A published post doesn't (its changes would go live): it waits for "Actualizează". Every change, to any post, is also kept on the device until it's saved; if the tab or the phone closes first, the editor offers it back ("Restaurează").
- **Series:** posts that belong together, in order. In the editor's settings: pick a series or create one, and a part (by default after the others). The post page shows the series above the text ("Seria … · Partea 2 din 4", with all the parts) and "Partea următoare" after it; at the end of the text the bottom bar's next article is the next part. Search engines get it as a `CreativeWorkSeries`.
- **Notifications** (the bell, top right; `/notificari`), what each account chose to hear about on its Settings page (`/setari`, "Setări" after "Profil" in the account menu), saved as they change:
  - **Members:** new posts (marked as such for the authors they follow), replies to their comments, reactions to their comments, being mentioned (@username) in a comment.
  - **Authors:** those, plus on their posts: likes, shares, comments, replies in the discussion, reactions to its comments, new views (one notification every N views, 10 unless changed), and milestones: the first, 10th, 50th, 100th and 1,000th comment (all comments count, replies and their own too), like and view, each one on or off.
  - **Administrators** (the "owner" role, called Administrator in the app): only the administration's: new support tickets, reported comments, comments waiting for approval, accounts to delete for good.
  - Never about one's own actions. Likes, shares and reactions are grouped while unread ("Ana și încă 3 au apreciat…"); each milestone comes once. The page lists them newest first, grouped by day, 20 at a time as you scroll, across the page's width; "Setări notificări" is a small button in its header. Filters (kept in the address), in one row: All / Unread (a switch with the unread count), the type (what the role gets), the post (a searchable list of the posts the notifications are about, loading as it scrolls; not for administrators) and a period (quick choices, or a first and last day on a calendar, each with a time; `components/DateRangePicker.tsx`). A field opens a panel under it on wide screens, a dialog at 1000px and narrower, a sheet from the bottom on phones; at 760px and narrower the row becomes two by two. Nothing is marked read by opening the page: the ✓ on each, "Marchează toate ca citite", or opening one does it.
  - **Signed out**, the bell opens a small panel to get new posts as notifications on this device (no page: there's no account).
- **Push notifications:** a device gets new posts while signed out (the bell's switch), and an account's notifications once "Notificări push pe acest dispozitiv" is on in Settings (with a test button). A new post is announced once, when it goes public (a scheduled one when its time comes): in the app to the accounts that want it, pushed to their devices and to the signed-out devices that asked. One that went public more than 7 days before it was ever announced (imported content, seeded demo posts) is old news: it's marked announced without telling anyone. Web Push with VAPID keys made on first start (`app_settings`) and the `web-push` library; the push services are the browsers' own (Google, Apple, Mozilla). It needs https (or localhost); on iPhones only in the installed app (iOS 16.4+). Set `PUSH_CONTACT` (a `mailto:` or `https:` address) for production.
- **Support** (`/suport`, "Suport" in the account menu, or in the settings menu signed out): a contact form in the middle of the page, under a centred title (on phones, Send spans the form) (email, optional name, a category, title, description, up to 3 attachments: images, PDF or text, 10 MB each, checked by their content). Tickets and their attachments are kept in the database (attachments never become public files); a hidden field catches robots. Administrators get a notification and read them in the admin panel (`/admin/suport`): new ones marked, the message, who sent it (and their account), pictures shown, other files to download, and "Răspunde pe email". No replies in the app yet.
- **Editor on phones and tablets** (980px and narrower): a full screen of its own. The site's top buttons give way to a bar that stays on top while writing: back, the post's status, ⚙ (the post's settings, in a sheet from the bottom: publishing, category, comments, excerpt, slug, delete) and the save or publish button. The formatting tools are one row under it that scrolls sideways, and their pop-ups (colours, links, video) open across the screen. On phones the text is edge to edge, without the card.
- **Post page:** the banner runs full width with the categories and the title on it (no summary: that's for the home page); the publish date, reading time and views (each visitor once a day) are just below it. On wide screens, "Editează" (only for the post's author and owners) sits at the right end of the "Înapoi la articole" line; on narrow screens (1000px and narrower) "Înapoi la articole" is in the top bar, left of its buttons, so the banner starts right under it; there the banner runs from edge to edge (not a card). Then the text sits beside a sticky sidebar with Like, Share and the comment count (three tiles), Save in a small card of its own, a contents list built from the article's headings, the author (picture, name, occupation or "Autor", quote), and "Citește și" (the same category first, then the latest posts). After the text, "Ți-a fost util?" offers Like and Share. At 1000px and narrower, the text uses the full width and the sidebar is hidden (so there's no contents list). Its actions move to an app-style bar fixed to the bottom of the screen: Like and Save on the left; on the right Share, and Edit for the post's author and owners or, for everyone else, the discussion with its comment count; and a round Home button in the middle that goes back to the articles. It sits half above the bar, in a notch with a 5px see-through ring, where a ring fills as the text is read. At the end of the text the round button turns into an arrow to the next article (the same category first), with an "Urmează" pill above the bar naming it. The bar slides away while scrolling down and comes back on the way up, near the top, at the end of the text, and while typing it's hidden. Double-tapping the banner likes the post, with a heart where the finger was; likes and saves give a short vibration on Android phones. The author (no card, centred) sits under "Ți-a fost util?", and "Citește și" becomes three wide cards above the discussion.
- **Discussion:** the header has the count and Populare / Recente sorting. Guests get a "join the discussion" card with Google sign-in; signed-in users get a composer. Every comment is a card on a connected reply tree. Replies nest up to three levels; replying deeper attaches to the third level. Emoji reactions (❤️ ✨ 🌙 🔥 😂 🙏): "Reacționează" opens a dock of emoji (on hover with a mouse, or a tap), each lifting with its name as you point at it; a chip on the right shows the most used ones and the total, and opens the breakdown. Threads load 10 at a time as you scroll (by Populare: reactions plus replies, or Recente), and each comment shows 5 replies, with "Vezi mai multe răspunsuri" for 5 more. Only one form is open at a time: opening a reply or an edit closes the other; replies use the same form as edits. New comments, replies, and showing or hiding a thread slide in and out. Threads can be collapsed. People edit their own comments (marked "editat") and delete them (a dialog asks first); owners can also remove anyone's. Nobody else can delete a comment, whatever their role. Deletes are soft and take the replies with them: the comments disappear, and owners still see them greyed out with a "Șters" badge (hover it for who deleted it and when). When the author turns comments off, the section says so and nothing is shown. Guests see reactions but sign in to react or reply. In the database each comment knows its thread (`root_id`, the top-level comment) and level (`depth`); an edit keeps the text it replaced (`comment_revisions`); each reaction is a row, and taking it back marks it removed instead of deleting it, so reactions have a history.
- **Discussion on phones** (640px and narrower), as on Facebook: the title and the sorting stay on one line, and the composer is a rounded field that grows as you type. Each comment's name and text sit in a grey bubble, with a row under it: how long ago ("3 h", "acum"), the react button (just its icon, or your emoji), "Răspunde", "⋯" on comments you can edit or delete (it opens a sheet from the bottom with Edit and Delete), and the reactions at the end. Threads stay open (no "Ascunde răspunsurile"). On a 360px screen the row can still wrap for your own comments three levels deep.
- **Admin panel** (`/admin`, "Administrare" in the account menu; administrators only): its own app in `client/src/admin/` with its own look (`admin.css`, light and dark themes of its own, a sidebar of sections, a top bar with language, full width / centred content, theme, notifications (the bell: support, reports, accounts to delete) and sign-out), loaded only when opened (each page is a lazy child route in `main.tsx`). **Panou**: the month's numbers against the month before, traffic by day, what needs attention (maintenance on, reports, comments to approve, new tickets, email not set up, deleted and suspended accounts, drafts, pending invitations) and the latest comments, accounts, most-read posts and tickets. **Statistici** (`/admin/statistici`): the whole site's, for 7/30/90 days, or one author's (`?author=`): main numbers with their change, a daily chart, interactions, every post's numbers (sortable), where visits come from, devices, what readers do, other pages, the community. **Articole** (`/admin/articole`): every post by status (and the pinned ones), search, author and order, with views, likes and comments; open, edit (the site's editor), pin to the home page, delete, restore. **Categorii** (`/admin/categorii`), with the tags under them. **Imagini** (`/admin/media`). **Comentarii** (`/admin/comentarii`): those waiting for approval first (why they wait; approve or reject), the reported ones (each report: reason, details, who), then all and deleted; keep, delete (with its replies), suspend the author. **Newsletter** (`/admin/newsletter`). **Utilizatori** (`/admin/utilizatori`): every account by status (active, suspended, deleted, unconfirmed), role and search; an account's panel has its numbers, role (member ↔ author), suspend / lift, restore a deleted account, mark the email confirmed, and its latest comments. **Suport** (`/admin/suport`). **Setări** (`/admin/setari`): the site's rules, its identity, the SMTP server (see Email) with a test email, and the email history. API: `server/src/routes/admin.ts` (`/api/admin/*`). Administrators don't save posts: no Save buttons, no saved list or its privacy switch (the server refuses too). Every menu is one component (`admin/Dropdown.tsx`): fixed choices, with a filter box for long lists; people from the database through `SearchSelect` / `UserSelect` (searched on the server a few at a time, `GET /api/admin/lookup/users`).
- **Email** (SMTP, set by administrators in the admin panel → Setări; kept in `app_settings` as `smtp`; `server/src/lib/mail.ts`, templates in `lib/emails.ts`, in the language the person was using): confirming an address, a new password, a new address, deleted / restored accounts, invitations to write, and the newsletter. In development without an SMTP server, emails are written to the server's log (with their links); in production without one, password recovery says it's unavailable and email changes apply at once, unconfirmed.
- **Site settings** (admin panel → Setări → General; `app_settings` `site`, `server/src/lib/siteSettings.ts`), every one off by default except the newsletter:
  - "Doar conturile cu emailul confirmat pot comenta": an account without a confirmed address sees a card with "send the link again" instead of the comment box (and no Reply); the server refuses its comments (`email_unverified`).
  - **Comment filters:** an account's first comment waits for approval; comments with links are allowed, wait, or are refused; a list of filtered words (matched without case or diacritics) makes a comment wait or refuses it (`comment_blocked`). A comment that waits is seen only by its writer (marked "Așteaptă aprobarea") and administrators, who get a notification and approve or reject it in Comentarii → De aprobat (approving sends the usual notifications then). An edit goes through the filters again. Administrators' and authors' own comments aren't filtered.
  - **Announcement:** a bar above every page (text, an optional link, a colour); readers can close it, and a new text shows again.
  - **Newsletter** on or off (see below).
  - **Maintenance:** the site closed to everyone but administrators: visitors get a "Revenim în curând" page (the message can be changed; pages, the sitemap and the RSS feed answer 503 to search engines), signing in still works, and administrators see the site with a reminder bar.
- **Email history** (Setări → Istoric emailuri; `email_log`): every email the site tried to send (to whom, which kind, subject, sent / failed with the server's error / only logged in development), kept 90 days; today's count against the SMTP server's daily limit (set with the SMTP settings; Gmail and Brevo presets fill it in). The dashboard warns about failures in the last 24 hours and from 80% of the limit.
- **Administrators' log** (`/admin/jurnal`, Sistem → Jurnal; `admin_log`, `server/src/lib/adminLog.ts`): who did what and when (roles, suspensions, restores, deletions for good, confirmed emails, deleted or kept comments, posts written, changed, moved, deleted or restored by an administrator, categories, settings, never a password), by day, filtered by administrator, kind and words; an account's panel shows its own history. Kept 2 years.
- **Tags:** besides its category, a post gets up to 10 tags in the editor (suggested from the tags already used as you type; the same words make one tag). They're shown under the post, link to `/eticheta/{slug}`, and go to search engines (`article:tag`). Administrators rename (onto another's name merges them) or delete them in Categorii → Etichete.
- **Following authors:** "Urmărește" under the author on a post (signed-out readers go to sign in). Their new posts then come as "{author}, pe care îl urmărești, a publicat…" (a setting of its own), and Settings lists the authors followed, to unfollow. Authors see how many follow them; nobody sees who.
- **Mentions:** typing @ in a comment suggests the post's author and the people in its discussion; a comment mentioning @username notifies them (a setting of its own), and mentions are highlighted.
- **Newsletter** (`server/src/lib/newsletter.ts`): a box on the home page, under posts and in the phone feed, and a card in Settings: an address (the account's, for someone signed in), new posts at once or a weekly digest (Monday 09:00 in the site's time zone). Confirmed by a link (unless it's the signed-in account's confirmed address); every email has a link to change or stop it (`/newsletter/{token}`) and a one-click unsubscribe header. Sent a few at a time each minute, only posts from the last 7 days, and never past 90% of the SMTP server's daily limit. Administrators see the subscribers (Newsletter in the admin panel) and can delete one; unconfirmed ones go after 30 days.
- **Version history:** before each editing session (one person's saves within 10 minutes) the post as it was is kept, 50 per post (`post_revisions`). The editor's "Istoric versiuni" lists them, compares one with the text in the editor (paragraphs gone and new) or shows it, and brings it back into the editor (saving then keeps what it replaced as a version too).
- **Preview links:** for a draft or a scheduled post, the editor makes a secret link (`/previzualizare/{token}`) that shows the saved post to anyone with it, without the discussion; "Oprește linkul" ends it (a new one replaces the old). Not for search engines.
- **SEO fields and image descriptions:** a title and a description for search results (with counters and a preview of the result) and an image for shares, each falling back to the title, the excerpt and the banner. Selecting a picture in the text shows a field for its description (alt text); the editor counts the pictures still without one.
- **Old links keep working:** changing a post's address keeps the old one leading to it (`post_redirects`; a 301 for search engines, and the app follows it too).
- **Invitations to write** (Utilizatori → "Invită un autor"): an email with a link (`/invitatie?token=…`, 14 days) and an optional message; with it, someone without an account makes one that's already an author's, and someone with one (signed in with that address) becomes an author. Resend or withdraw it from the list under the accounts.
- **Media library** (admin panel → Imagini): every picture posts were given, with its size and the posts using it; the unused ones (not in any post nor in an earlier version, and older than a day) are deleted one by one or all at once.
- **Site identity** (Setări → Identitate; `app_settings` `identity`, `server/src/lib/siteIdentity.ts`): the site's name, company, description, default language for new visitors, time zone, contact address, social links and logo. Every page carries it from the server (`window.__SITE__`), so a change shows on the next page load, and the icons, favicon and share images follow a new logo. `site.config.ts` only holds the defaults.
- **Search insights** (Statistici, the whole site): what readers searched for on the home page and what found nothing, counted per day with nothing about who searched (`search_log`, kept 25 months).
- **First run:** a fresh copy with no administrator asks, on any page, for the site's name and description and the first administrator's account. The server prints a setup code in its log at start-up, which the page asks for, so only whoever runs the server can do it.
- **Confirmed emails:** a new email/password account gets a link to confirm its address (`/confirmare-email`); Profile shows whether it's confirmed, with "send the link again". A Google sign-in confirms it. A new address (Profile) changes only once its link is followed (Profile shows it waiting, with "Renunță"). Signing in with Google to an account whose address was never confirmed takes it over: its password is removed and it's signed out everywhere else (whoever made it never proved the address was theirs). Register and email changes refuse an address any account has, deleted ones included.
- **Forgotten password:** "Ai uitat parola?" on `/login` → `/parola-uitata` (the same answer whether or not the address has an account) → an emailed link to `/resetare-parola` (one hour, once); a new password confirms the address and signs out every other browser.
- **Deleting an account** (Profile, last card; not administrators): a dialog says what happens, asks what to do with their comments (stay under "Cont șters", or deleted with the replies under them) and, for someone with posts, with the posts (stay, or deleted), then the password (if the account has one) and a tick. The account is signed out everywhere and can't sign in: signing in says from which day it can be deleted for good, and to send a support ticket if it was a mistake. What they chose to delete disappears at once (marked `deleted_with_account`); restoring the account (administrators, Utilizatori or the support ticket from its address) brings it all back. It's kept at least 3 months; then administrators are told (the bell, push, the dashboard's "De șters definitiv" card) and delete it for good themselves, never automatically (`finalizeAccountDeletion`): the chosen comments and posts go for good (with the posts' images), kept posts move to an author picked then, and everything that identifies the person goes; the address is free again. An hourly job (`accountHousekeeping`) sends those notices and drops support tickets after 2 years, statistics and search counts after 25 months, unconfirmed newsletter subscriptions after 30 days, and invitations after a year (what the Terms say). Deleting an account also stops its newsletter; deleting it for good removes its follows and subscription.
- **Suspensions** (administrators, Utilizatori or Comentarii): for 1–90 days or until lifted, with a reason. The person is signed out everywhere and gets an email; signing in says until when, and why.
- **Reporting comments:** signed-in readers report someone else's comment (the flag icon, or "⋯" → Raportează on phones): a reason (spam, harassment, hateful, inappropriate, something else) and optional details. One open report per person and comment. Administrators get a notification (grouped per comment while unread) and the report queues in the admin panel; deleting the comment settles it.
- **Cookie banner** (`ConsentBanner`, `lib/consent.ts`): "Accept statisticile", "Doar cele necesare" or a choice per category; kept 6 months in the `consent` cookie, which the server reads. Nothing is measured before a choice. Without consent to statistics there's no visitor cookie or fingerprint: a post only gets one more view on its public counter (once per visit while the site is open), shares still tell the author, and a guest's like sets the visitor cookie (needed to remember it). The visitor cookie lasts 13 months.
- **Your data** (Profile, "Descarcă datele mele"): everything kept about the account as a JSON file (`GET /api/me/export`, `server/src/lib/dataExport.ts`): profile, comments and their earlier versions, reactions, likes, saved posts, posts, reports, support tickets, notifications, devices, sessions, the authors followed, the newsletter subscription, and the statistics linked to the account.
- **Fonts:** Inter and Fraunces are served by the site itself (`client/src/styles/fonts.css`, files in `styles/fonts/`, SIL Open Font License), Latin and Latin Extended; nothing is requested from Google Fonts.
- **Terms and Conditions** (`/termeni-si-conditii`, text in `client/src/pages/termsContent.ts`, Romanian and English): the rules, moderation and suspensions, what is stored and why, cookies, outside services, how long data is kept, deleting an account, GDPR rights. Keep it in step with the code when what's stored changes.
- **Author statistics** (`/statistici`, "Statistici" in the cog menu and the phone's bottom bar; authors): the same statistics for their own posts only, without the site's other pages and the community; "Înapoi la articole" in the top bar. Administrators going there land on `/admin/statistici`.
  - Statistics, for 7, 30 or 90 days, compared with the days before (the server sends both periods' totals): four main numbers (views, visitors, average reading time, read to the end), each with how it changed ("▲ 12%", "▼ 3 p.p.", "nou") and, for views and visitors, a small line of the days; views and visitors by day, on an even scale, with each day's numbers when pointed at or touched; interactions beside it (likes, comments and replies, shares, saves, each with its change, and how long people read before liking or commenting); the posts, most viewed first, with a bar for their views and their numbers in columns (a line with icons on phones); what readers do (each bar button, each share app, double taps, installs), where visits come from, devices (a ring); the site's other pages and the community (administrators, the whole site).
  - What's recorded: each page opened (one row per visitor, page and day: opening it again only adds to its count), active reading time (the tab visible and the reader active in the last 45 seconds) and how far into the post, sent every 15 seconds and as the page closes; and actions with the reading time before them (one row per visitor, action, page and day, with how many times). Likes and comments come from their own tables.
  - A visitor is a random first-party cookie (`vid`) and a fingerprint: a salted hash (HMAC) of the IP address, browser and language. Either one dedupes, so clearing cookies doesn't count twice. IP addresses aren't stored, only the hash; the salt is generated in the database (`app_settings`). Crawlers, link previews and automated browsers aren't counted and can't like. Being in the EU, mention this in the privacy policy (the terms page is still a placeholder).
- **Installable app:** a web app manifest and icons made from the logo (`/manifest.webmanifest`, `/icons/*`; the app opens on the midnight colours), and a service worker in production (`client/public/sw.js`) that keeps built files and images and the last copy of each page read, so posts already read open offline. From the second visit, after 20 seconds of reading, a card offers to add the site to the home screen: Chrome's own install dialog on Android and desktop, the two steps (Share, then "Add to Home Screen") on iPhones. Not again for a month after "Nu acum". The phone's status bar takes the theme's background. Browsers only install sites served over `https` (or `localhost`), so not from the local-network address in development.
- **Page width:** every page uses the same container, 1240px with 1192px of content.
- **Search engines:** every page's HTML already has its title, description, canonical address and robots rule. Posts also get Open Graph and X tags with dates, author and category, schema.org data (`BlogPosting` and a breadcrumb), and the article itself, so it's indexed without running JavaScript and shows before the app has loaded. The home page gets `WebSite` (with the search box) and `Organization` data, and category pages their own title and description. Searches, sign-in and personal pages are `noindex`, and unknown addresses answer 404. `/sitemap.xml` lists every published post (when it last changed, and its banner), the categories and the terms page; `/robots.txt` points to it, and `/rss.xml` has the latest posts. Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console once the site is live.
- **Sharing:** link previews (WhatsApp, Facebook, X, Telegram, Slack…) show each post's share image: its banner with the site name, the category badge and the title over it, 1200 × 630, generated on the server and cached until the title, banner or category changes. "Distribuie" opens a share sheet with that preview, WhatsApp, Facebook, X, Telegram, Pinterest and email, copying the link, and the phone's own share menu. It's a dialog on wide screens. On phones it's a compact sheet rising from the bottom that fits without scrolling and, like the comment options sheet, can be swiped down to close (past a third of its height or with a flick; otherwise it springs back). While any dialog or sheet is open, the page behind it doesn't scroll. On phones it also offers Instagram, which has no share link: the picture goes through the phone's share menu, and the link is copied for a story sticker.
- **Categories** (`/admin/categorii` in the admin panel): a tree up to 3 levels deep (category → sub → sub-sub) with at most 10 children per parent, plus astrology symbols. The limits are in `server/src/config.ts` (`categoryLimits`).
- **Preferences popover** (top right): Light / Dark / Midnight theme (Midnight by default) and Română / English.

## Setup

1. In Google Cloud Console, open your OAuth client and add **Authorized redirect URI** `http://localhost:3000/api/auth/google/callback`. While the app's publishing status is **Testing**, add your Gmail under **Audience → Test users**. Otherwise Google answers `access_denied`.
2. Run:

```sh
npm install
cp server/.env.example server/.env   # fill in the Google client ID/secret and OWNER_EMAILS
npm run db                            # MySQL in Docker
npm run dev                           # API + client
```

Open http://localhost:5173. Vite also listens on the local network, so a phone on the same Wi-Fi can open `http://<this computer's IP>:5173` (Vite prints it as "Network"). Google sign-in only works on `localhost` (Google doesn't accept local network addresses as redirect URIs), so use email and password there. Database migrations run automatically on server start (`server/src/migrations.ts`). On an empty database (no administrator yet), the site opens on its first-run setup, which asks for the setup code printed in the server's log. Uploaded images go to `server/uploads/` (generated share images and app icons in `server/uploads/og/`, a cache).

**Production:** build the client (`npm run build -w client`), set `APP_URL` in `server/.env` to the public `https://` address, and run the server with `NODE_ENV=production` (`npm run start -w server`). It then serves the built app too, putting each page's title, tags and (for posts) content into the HTML, as Vite does in development. Behind a reverse proxy on another machine, set `TRUST_PROXY` to its address so statistics see visitors' real IPs (a proxy on the same machine is trusted by default).

## Giving someone Administrator access

Administrators (the `owner` role, called "Administrator" on the site) get the admin panel, `/admin`. The panel's
Users page only switches member ↔ author; Administrator access is given outside the site, in one of three ways:

1. **By email, in `server/.env`** (the usual way): add the address to `OWNER_EMAILS` (comma-separated, e.g.
   `OWNER_EMAILS=ana@example.com,ion@example.com`) and restart the server. The account with that address becomes an
   Administrator the next time it signs in. Such an address can't sign up with a password (anyone could type it in),
   so the person signs in with Google; someone who already has an account with a password just signs in again.
2. **In the database**, for an account that already exists (at once, no restart, no new sign-in needed):

   ```sh
   docker exec -it newfolder3-mysql-1 mysql -uapp -papp_password jurnal_production
   ```

   ```sql
   UPDATE users SET role = 'owner' WHERE email = 'ana@example.com';
   ```

   (`jurnal_production` is `production/`'s database, `google_login` is `local/`'s; `SELECT id, name, email, role FROM
   users;` lists the accounts.)
3. **On a fresh database** (no Administrator yet), the site opens on its first-run setup, which creates the first
   Administrator; it asks for the setup code printed in the server's log at start-up.

To take it away: remove the address from `OWNER_EMAILS` if it's there (or it comes back at the next sign-in), then
`UPDATE users SET role = 'member' WHERE email = 'ana@example.com';` (or `'author'`).

Administrators write posts only in an author's name, don't save posts, and can't delete their own account (another
Administrator can't either: it's done in the database, by turning them back into a member first).

## Local and production

The project lives twice, side by side: `local/` is where it's developed (`npm run dev`, http://localhost:5173), `production/` is the running site, updated only when asked to (copying the changes over from `local/`).

- **Start production:** `production/start.cmd` (double-click), or `production/start.ps1` from PowerShell. It installs dependencies the first time, builds the site, and runs the server with `NODE_ENV=production` on **port 4400 on every network interface** (http://localhost:4400, or http://<this computer's IP>:4400 from a phone on the same network). The one server serves both the site and the API. Stop it with Ctrl+C.
- Production has its own database (`jurnal_production`, on the same MySQL as `local/`, which must be running) and its own uploads (`production/server/uploads`), and its own `production/server/.env`.
- **Google sign-in** in production needs its redirect address added to the Google OAuth client: `http://localhost:4400/api/auth/google/callback` (and the real domain's, once there is one). Email and password work regardless.
- On a real domain: put it in `APP_URL` (https), which also turns on secure cookies, and set `PUSH_CONTACT`. Service workers, installing the app and push notifications need https (or localhost).

## Code map

- `site.config.ts` (repo root): the defaults for the site's identity (name, company, description, languages, time zone); what administrators set in Setări → Identitate replaces them (`server/src/lib/siteIdentity.ts`), and the client reads the current one through `client/src/config.ts` (`site`, and `siteSettings`: announcement, maintenance, newsletter, first run).
- `server/src/routes/`: `auth` (Google + email/password, forgotten password, email links), `me` (profile, profile picture, email changes, deleting the account, the viewer's likes and bookmarks), `admin` (the admin panel's API: overview, people, suspensions, moderation, posts, SMTP), `posts` (also public likes), `categories`, `comments` (replies, edits, soft deletes, emoji reactions), `uploads`, `track` (what browsers report for the statistics), `stats` (the admin dashboard's data), `notifications`, `push` (devices' push choices), `series`, `pwa` (web app manifest, icons, favicon and the splash's logo, at the root; made from `assets/logo.png` by `seo/og.ts`, versioned in `seo/brand.ts`)
- `server/src/seo/`: what each page's HTML says before the app runs (`page.ts`), putting it into `index.html` (`html.ts`, also used by Vite in development), and the share images (`og.ts`, drawn with the site's fonts from `server/assets/fonts`, SIL Open Font License). `server/src/routes/feeds.ts`: robots.txt, sitemap.xml, rss.xml
- `server/src/lib/`: sessions, guards (`requireUser` / `requireWriter` / `requireOwner`, `canEditPost`), post visibility per role (`visibleTo`), image uploads (WebP conversion in `uploads.ts`), HTML sanitizing, slugs, the category tree; `visitor.ts` (visitor cookie and fingerprint), `analytics.ts` (recording views, reading time and actions), `likes.ts` (public likes and their history), `comments.ts` (storing a comment with its thread and depth), `notifications.ts` (notifying authors, announcing new posts), `push.ts` (VAPID keys, sending pushes)
- `client/src/pages/`: Home, Post, Auth (login/register), Saved, Profile, Terms, Editor, ManagePosts, Admin (also authors' `StatsPage`), Notifications, Categories, Users; `StatusPages` has `RequireRole` (user / writer / owner)
- `client/src/components/`: `Header` (the top bar; `lib/headerSlot.ts` lets a page put a control in it), `AccountSheet` (phones' account menu and settings), `Modal` (dialogs, and sheets from the bottom or top that swipe away), `PostCard` (cards, and `FeedPost` for the phone feed), `ShareSheet` (with `ShareButton`)
- `client/src/lib/analytics.ts`: page views, reading time and actions sent to the server (`trackPage`, `track`); `lib/toast.ts` and `components/Toaster.tsx`: short messages at the bottom; `components/InstallPrompt.tsx`: the add-to-home-screen card; `public/sw.js`: the service worker
- `client/src/context/`: auth, categories, reactions (likes for everyone, through the server; bookmarks for members, and in the browser for guests)
- `client/src/lib/i18n/messages.ts`: all UI text (Romanian is the source; English must define the same keys)
- `client/src/styles/`: theme tokens (`base.css`), components, pages, editor/prose
